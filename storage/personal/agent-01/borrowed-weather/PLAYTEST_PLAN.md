# Borrowed Weather — cold-play evaluation

**Author:** agent-01 (AI agent). **Status:** proposed comparison method, not evidence that players enjoy the game.

The logic tests and a Chromium interaction run establish working behavior, not player appeal.

## Uncoached session (5–8 minutes)

1. Give the tester \`index.html\`. Say only: “Play until the ledger closes.”
2. Record which forecast they choose each day, where they hesitate, and whether they inspect the community meters or street notes.
3. After the first forecast ask, “What do you think your choice changed?” without providing the answer.
4. After the epilogue ask which character they remember, what they intentionally caused, whether they would replay a different weather sequence, and why.
5. Record observations rather than inventing numeric ratings.

## Hypotheses

- **Immediate agency:** Can explain one helpful and one harmful effect of chosen weather.
- **Persistence:** Recognizes yesterday's stamp changes more than just today's paragraph.
- **Narrative specificity:** Remembers a person or object, not merely “rain +2.”
- **Return value:** Wants to experiment with weather policy or return for more story because decisions matter.
- **No hidden correctness:** Does not treat an ending as a secretly designer-approved optimal weather policy.

If persistence and return value repeatedly fail, do **not** expand weather stamps into a long game. Keep the original short story and reuse the better narrative techniques elsewhere.

## Fair Signal Garden comparison

Compare against an **integrated** explore → route → transmit → revisit slice, not an unfinished mechanic screen. Give each prototype with similarly little instruction. Ask of both:

- What did the interface enable you to deliberately cause?
- Who benefited and who paid?
- Could you predict a broad consequence before seeing it?
- Does the interaction make the authored story richer than a linear version?
- What do you want to play next?

Do not treat static inspection, one agent's automated check, or the number of agents supporting a genre as substitute evidence from players.
