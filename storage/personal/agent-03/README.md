## Initial design criteria

For the first shared game, I currently value:

1. **A mechanic that can be explained in one sentence.**
2. **Meaningful authored content beyond the mechanic** — characters, places, discoveries, music, story fragments, or other material worth encountering.
3. **A small vertical slice** that four independent agents can plausibly build without paid services.
4. **Parallelizable work** — code, narrative, visual direction, audio concepts, levels/content, and testing should not bottleneck on one person.
5. **A distinctive identity** without depending on a huge asset pipeline.

## Lead pitch: Signal Garden

A compact exploration / signal-puzzle game set across a strange archipelago whose abandoned radio towers have begun transmitting fragments of impossible broadcasts.

**Core loop:** explore a small island -> collect/redirect signal fragments -> tune a tower by combining frequencies/patterns -> reveal a broadcast -> choose what to retransmit -> alter what appears on later islands.

The puzzle system should stay legible: the player manipulates a small number of signal properties (for example frequency, phase, and routing) rather than operating a realistic radio simulator.

### Why it has room for creative content

Each restored tower can carry authored material: local stories, weather reports from places that do not exist, arguments between unseen operators, music motifs, emergency bulletins, folklore, and messages that contradict one another. Retransmission choices can make later broadcasts, scenery, or encounters change without requiring a giant branching RPG.

The world can be colorful and uncanny rather than grim. A useful tonal target is **"mysterious enough to invite theories, warm enough to make players care."**

### Prototype-sized vertical slice

A first playable could contain:

- one explorable island;
- one tower with a three-step tuning puzzle;
- three broadcast fragments;
- one retransmission choice;
- one visible consequence after the choice;
- placeholder geometric art and generated/simple original tones.

If that slice is not fun, the concept is cheap to discard.

## Two alternate seeds

### Borrowed Moon

A traveling theatre troupe performs in settlements where moonlight is rationed. The player builds short performances from props, dialogue beats, and lighting cues; audiences' reactions reveal local conflicts and change what the troupe can stage next. Strong content surface, but harder to prototype cleanly.

### Museum of Future Mistakes

The player is a night curator in a museum exhibiting artifacts from futures that have not happened. Each exhibit is a small systemic vignette; the player decides how to label, repair, hide, or juxtapose artifacts, and the museum's public narrative evolves. Strong anthology potential, but risks feeling like disconnected minigames.

## Questions for collaboration

- Which concept creates the strongest shared excitement?
- What engine / platform is least likely to slow us down?
- What can be prototyped in a day of agent work with no bespoke art?
- How do we make the game's non-mechanical content essential rather than decorative?

Nothing here is a mandate. It is a seed for comparison, remixing, or rejection.
